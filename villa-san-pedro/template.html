BRIEF

A family home for a sculptor, his wife, their four children and Matt, the dog, on an 800 m² rectangular plot of 25.00 x 32.00 m with its short side on the street.

The sculptor works with heavy, dusty and noisy materials: stone, wood and clay. The studio has to take deliveries from the street and give him light and height. It also has to stay far enough from the bedrooms that dust and noise never reach the family.

  • Parents: suite with walk-through dressing, bathroom and a drawing studio overlooking the patio.
  • 4 children: four bedrooms of about 12 m², two shared bathrooms, a family room and a homework library.
  • Sculptor: 6 m high studio with north light, a mezzanine, a tool store and an outdoor work yard with a hoist.
  • Matt: a kennel and shaded run on the service alley, with a gate to the garden and a dog shower.
  • Household: guest room, two kitchens, laundry, a staff room, a 2-car carport and an 11 m pool.

WEST · ART

The studio sits on the street and on the west boundary. Deliveries come straight in through a 4.50 m gate and a 2 t monorail hoist. Its blind west wall shields the house from the low afternoon sun, and three north-facing sheds give it even daylight.

CENTRE · FAMILY

A covered gallery of the sculptor's work leads from the gate to the front door. The living room runs right through the house, from the patio on one side to the terrace and pool on the other. Upstairs, all bedrooms face south over the garden, behind a balcony and wooden shutters.

EAST · SERVICE

The carport, staff room, laundry and Ivorian kitchen open onto a service alley along the east boundary. Staff and deliveries never cross the living rooms. Matt guards the alley and joins the children in the garden.

CLIMATE AND MATERIALS hot, humid coastal climate, latitude 4.7° N

  • Cross ventilation: the living band is one room deep. The south-west sea breeze crosses the terrace and the living room, and warm air leaves through the patio.
  • Sun: the long façades face north and south. A 1.50 m balcony and a 2.50 m roof overhang shade the south glazing. East and west walls are blind or screened.
  • Studio light: three north-facing sheds give even, glare-free light for modelling, with no direct sun on the work.
  • Rain: roofs drain into a buried 20 m³ cistern under the carport for the garden and the studio wash-down.
  • Structure: reinforced-concrete frame and slabs on a 4.50 / 8.00 m bay rhythm. Ground floor 3.35 m clear, R+1 3.00 m, studio 6.00 m.
  • Walls: stabilised compressed-earth blocks (BTC) for the studio and the street front. Rendered blocks for the house.
  • Screens: terracotta claustra for the patio, the work yard and the kitchen. Iroko louvred shutters on the bedrooms.
  • Coastal durability: hot-dip galvanised steel, stainless fixings and marine-grade aluminium joinery, because the site is near the sea.

ASSUMPTIONS TO CONFIRM

  • Orientation: the street is assumed to be on the north side. If it is on the south, the scheme flips: the studio stays on the street and the bedrooms keep their garden side.
  • Plot proportions: 25.00 m of street frontage by 32.00 m deep. A 20 x 40 m plot would need the bands reorganised.
  • Planning rules: the scheme assumes a 5.00 m front setback and blind walls built on the west boundary. Both need checking against the San-Pédro rules for this lotissement.
  • Ground: a soil survey is needed before foundations are designed. The pool and the cistern depend on the water table, and coastal sand is likely.
  • Children's rooms: four rooms of about 12 m² are planned. Two shared double rooms of 16 m² are an alternative that frees space for a playroom.
